U.S. master’s admissions: a complete introduction

From program fit and application materials to budgeting and preparing for your arrival.

Start with your goals, then your program list.

Decide what you want to learn, what kinds of roles interest you and what you can invest in the degree.

  • Compare curriculum, location, duration and total cost.
  • Check whether your background meets the program’s prerequisites.
  • For a career change, identify the specific preparation you still need.

Understand the curriculum behind the degree title.

MA, MS, MEng, MPS and MBA titles alone do not tell you how a program is taught.

  • Review course requirements, electives and capstone or thesis expectations.
  • Compare computer science, engineering, data science and business analytics by the skills taught.
  • Ask whether students from other disciplines are eligible and what prerequisite courses are required.

Build clear applications from your actual experience.

Each program sets its own requirements; use the current application instructions as your checklist.

  • Review transcripts, tests, essays, résumé and recommendation requirements.
  • Develop essays around your genuine interests, preparation and reasons for choosing the program.
  • Allow recommenders enough time and confirm how they must submit their letters.

Plan back from each deadline.

Use the timeline as a starting point and adjust it to the programs you choose.

  • Research programs and background preparation before drafting materials.
  • Track separate deadlines for applications, tests, recommendations and scholarships.
  • Review forms and uploaded documents before submission, then monitor your application portal.

Budget for the whole degree, not just one year.

Annual tuition is only part of the picture. Confirm the school’s current cost information and your own living arrangements.

  • Include tuition, mandatory fees, insurance, housing, food and transport.
  • Account for the program’s full length, application costs and travel.
  • Keep estimated or unconfirmed scholarships separate from funds you already have.

After admission, confirm the next steps.

Compare offers and conditions before making enrollment commitments.

  • Check acceptance and deposit deadlines with the university.
  • Follow official instructions for enrollment documents, financial evidence and visa preparation.
  • Research housing, insurance, arrival arrangements and international student support.

Connect application strategy with your next step.

IvyBridge focuses on individualized master’s planning grounded in your academic preparation and professional direction.

  • Profile assessment and a reasoned program shortlist.
  • Essay brainstorming, structure and review based on authentic experience.
  • Clear responsibilities, agreed deliverables and preparation for program interviews.

Questions to clarify before you apply.

Requirements differ across programs, even at the same university.

  • Changing fields: check required preparation rather than assuming every program accepts other backgrounds.
  • Tests: confirm current GRE, GMAT and English-language requirements for each application.
  • Practical training and visas: eligibility is individual; confirm current rules with the school’s DSO and official authorities.
